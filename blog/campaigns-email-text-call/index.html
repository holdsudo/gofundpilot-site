Multi-Channel Campaigns That Stop at the Right Moment

The best follow-up sequence is the one that knows when to go quiet. Here is how to design text, email and call campaigns that end exactly when they should.

A merchant replies to your text at 10:15 in the morning: yes, send the application. At 2:00 that afternoon, your automated sequence emails them a reminder that you have not heard back. At 6:30 the next morning their time, because the sequence was built on your clock rather than theirs, they get another text. By then they have decided your company is a nuisance.

Nobody designed that outcome. It came from a sequence that knew how to start but not how to stop. On a funding floor, follow-up campaigns are useful and often necessary. Merchants are busy and a single message is easy to miss. But a sequence that keeps going after a reply, or messages people at the wrong hour, does more harm than good.

This guide is about building campaigns around their exits as much as their steps.

Pre-flight summary

  • Design exit triggers before writing the first message.
  • Send by the merchant's local time and confirm outreach rules with counsel.
  • Use texts, emails and calls for their own strengths, not as copies of each other.
  • Stopping on funding protects the renewal relationship.
  • Watch replies, stage movement and opt-outs, not open rates.

Leg 01Design the stop conditions first

Most people building a campaign start with the first message. Try starting with the last one instead: under what circumstances should this sequence end immediately? Write those down before you draft a word.

In a funding context, a few exit triggers are nearly universal. Anything that signals a human conversation has begun, or the deal has moved on, should halt automation for that merchant.

  • The merchant replies by text or email, whatever they say
  • The merchant answers a call from a broker
  • The deal moves to a later stage, such as documents received or submitted
  • The deal funds
  • The merchant asks not to be contacted, which should end all outreach and be recorded

Leg 02Respect the merchant's clock, not yours

If you work merchants across time zones, a message scheduled for 9:00 in your office lands at 6:00 for someone three zones west. Campaigns should send based on the merchant's local time, inside the hours you have decided are appropriate.

Timing rules also have a compliance side. Rules around commercial texting and calling, including permitted hours, consent and opt-outs, vary and change, and they are your responsibility. Confirm current requirements with qualified counsel before running automated outreach, and build your campaigns to those requirements rather than to what feels convenient.

A sequence that cannot stop is not a follow-up plan. It is a complaint waiting to happen.

Leg 03Mix channels by what each one is good at

Texts, emails and calls do different jobs, and a good sequence uses each where it fits rather than repeating the same message in three formats.

Texts are short and personal: a quick check-in, a link to the upload portal, a reminder that you are holding a spot for their call. Emails carry detail: an offer summary, the list of documents still needed, an explanation of how a payment schedule works. Calls are for conversation, and in an automated sequence the right step is usually to create a task that puts the merchant in front of a broker in the power dialer, not to dial blindly.

Say a merchant downloaded an application but did not finish it. A reasonable sequence might be a text the same afternoon offering help, an email the next morning listing exactly what is left, a call task for a broker that afternoon, and one final text two days later. If they reply at any step, everything after it stops and the broker takes over.

When the call step comes up, FundPilot's power dialer can put the merchant in front of a broker with the full deal history on screen, including which texts and emails the sequence already sent, so the broker does not repeat what the merchant has already read.

Leg 04Building a sequence step by step

Here is a practical order of work for setting up a new campaign on a funding floor.

  1. Define who enters the sequence, such as merchants with an incomplete application older than one day.
  2. List the exit triggers and confirm each is tracked automatically on the deal.
  3. Set sending windows based on the merchant's local time.
  4. Draft each message with the specific next step, not a generic nudge.
  5. Space the steps out so the merchant has time to respond.
  6. Run the sequence on a small group first and read every reply.
  7. Review results after a few weeks and remove steps that never get responses.

Leg 05Why stopping on funding matters most

The most embarrassing campaign failure is messaging a merchant who already funded. Picture a merchant receiving a cheerful reminder that their application is incomplete the morning after the money landed. It tells them your systems do not talk to each other, and it undercuts every message you send them in future, including the renewal conversation months later.

This is where campaigns living inside the CRM, rather than in a separate marketing tool, pay off. In FundPilot, campaigns run text, email and call sequences that respect local time and stop on reply or funding, because they read the same deal record the brokers work from.

Leg 06Measuring what matters

Open rates are a weak signal in this business. The measures worth watching are replies that turn into conversations, merchants who move to the next stage after the sequence, and opt-out requests. A rising opt-out rate on a particular sequence is a clear sign that it is too frequent, too generic or reaching the wrong people.

Read the replies themselves, too. They tell you more about whether your messages make sense to a busy business owner than any dashboard. Adjust wording based on what merchants actually ask, and keep the tone calm and helpful rather than urgent.

Questions from the floor

How many steps should a follow-up sequence have?

There is no correct number, and more is not better. Start with a short sequence of a few steps spread over several days, run it on a small group, and read the replies. If later steps never produce responses, remove them. A sequence that is too long tends to generate opt-outs rather than conversations.

Should automated calls be part of a campaign?

In most cases the call step should put the merchant in front of a broker rather than play a recording, for example by queueing them in the power dialer. Automated and prerecorded calls are an area where rules are strict and vary, so consult qualified counsel about what is appropriate before including any automated calling.

What should happen when a merchant asks to stop?

All outreach to that contact should stop across every channel, and the request should be recorded on the merchant record so no future campaign or broker contacts them by mistake. Treat opt-out handling as a requirement of every sequence, and confirm with qualified counsel how your opt-out process should work for each channel.
