The 15-Minute Daily Huddle That Keeps a Funding Floor Moving

A short, fixed-agenda morning meeting that surfaces stuck deals, sets the day's priorities and gets brokers on the phones before nine-thirty.

It is 9:05. Half the floor is still pouring coffee, two brokers are already on calls with merchants in an earlier time zone, and your best closer has a contract out that needs a verification call before noon. If the morning meeting runs forty minutes and covers last month's leaderboard, you have just traded the most productive calling hour of the day for a recap nobody needed.

A good huddle is the opposite. It is short, it happens at the same minute every day, and it ends with every broker knowing exactly which deals they will move before lunch. Below is an agenda that fits in fifteen minutes, the handful of numbers worth putting on the screen, the habits that quietly ruin huddles, and how to run one when half your team is dialing from home.

Pre-flight summary

  • Same time, same agenda, fifteen minutes, every business day.
  • Talk about exceptions, not the whole pipeline.
  • Show only numbers someone can change before lunch.
  • Remote brokers get the identical meeting, on camera, with actions posted in chat.
  • Preparation should take five minutes, driven by saved views and stage limits.

Leg 01Why fifteen minutes, and why every day

Funding deals move in days, not quarters. A file that sits overnight without a statement request, a lender reply that goes unanswered until Thursday, an offer the merchant has not heard about yet: these are the small delays that turn a fundable deal into a merchant who signed elsewhere. A daily meeting catches them while they are still small.

The time limit matters as much as the frequency. Fifteen minutes forces you to talk about exceptions instead of narrating the whole pipeline, and it keeps the meeting cheap enough that nobody resents it. If something needs a longer conversation, take it offline with the two people involved.

Leg 02The agenda, minute by minute

Keep the order identical every day. Predictability is what lets brokers prepare in two minutes rather than twenty.

  1. Minutes 0 to 2: yesterday in one line. Funded count, new applications, submissions sent. No commentary.
  2. Minutes 2 to 7: stuck deals. The manager reads out deals that have sat in a stage past its limit. Each owner gives one sentence and one next action.
  3. Minutes 7 to 10: today's must-close list. Contracts out, offers to present, stipulations due. Owners confirm a time.
  4. Minutes 10 to 12: lender notes. Any guideline change, slow responder, or new appetite someone heard about yesterday.
  5. Minutes 12 to 14: one coaching point. A single short clip, phrase or technique, never a lecture.
  6. Minute 15: dial.

Leg 03Numbers worth putting on the screen

Show a few numbers that someone in the room can change today. Totals for the month are fine for a weekly review, but in a morning huddle they only invite either complacency or gloom.

Say your screen shows that yesterday the floor made plenty of dials but only a small share of new leads were reached within the first hour. That is actionable: the opening block today goes to fresh leads first. A month-to-date funded figure, by contrast, tells nobody what to do at 9:20.

  • Deals past their stage time limit, listed by owner
  • New leads from the last 24 hours with no contact attempt
  • Applications received but missing statements
  • Submissions with no lender reply after your agreed window
  • Contracts out and their expected verification time

Put on the screen only what someone in the room can change before lunch.

Leg 04What quietly ruins a huddle

Most failed huddles die from the same handful of habits. None of them looks harmful on a single day, which is why they creep in.

  • Public ranking of brokers by volume every morning, which rewards stacking low-quality submissions
  • Problem-solving a single deal in front of everyone while ten people wait
  • Starting late for stragglers, which teaches everyone that start time is a suggestion
  • Reading the full pipeline aloud instead of the exceptions
  • Turning the coaching minute into criticism of one person's call
  • Letting it run long because the manager is enjoying the conversation

Leg 05Running it with remote and hybrid brokers

Remote huddles need slightly more structure, not less. Start the video call two minutes early, share one screen with the exception list, and ask everyone to keep cameras on for the fifteen minutes. Call on people by name for stuck deals so nobody hides behind a muted microphone.

Keep the same agenda for everyone. A hybrid floor where the in-room brokers get a richer meeting than the remote ones will drift into two teams. If anyone is in a very different time zone, record the huddle and post the action list in team chat within five minutes.

FundPilot includes video calls and team chat inside the CRM, so the meeting, the exception list and the deals it refers to are all in one place. A broker who joins from home can click from the stuck-deal list straight into the record without hunting through another app.

Leg 06Preparing the huddle in five minutes

The manager's preparation should be mechanical. If building the stuck-deal list takes half an hour of spreadsheet work, the huddle will be skipped on busy days, which are exactly the days it matters most.

Set time limits on each pipeline stage and save a view that shows only deals past their limit. In FundPilot you can also ask the AI assistant for a plain-English summary, such as which applications came in yesterday without statements, and it answers from live CRM data. Read the list once, note the two or three deals that need a real conversation, and walk in.

After the meeting, post the must-close list and owners in team chat. The next morning, start by checking it. That loop, more than any agenda, is what turns a huddle into a habit that moves money. Questions from the floor

What time should a funding team hold its huddle?

Pick a time just after most brokers arrive and before the strongest calling window opens. For many floors that is a few minutes after the official start, but if your merchants cluster in another time zone, adjust. The best time is one you can protect every day. Moving it around to suit the manager's calendar teaches the team that the meeting is optional.

Should the owner attend the daily huddle?

Occasionally, as a listener. When an owner attends every day, brokers tend to perform for the boss rather than report problems honestly, and stuck deals get described more optimistically than they are. Let the floor manager run it. The owner gets more accurate information from the weekly review and from reading the exception list directly in the CRM.

How do I keep the huddle from running long?

Use a visible timer and a parking lot. When a stuck deal needs more than one sentence, the manager says it goes to the parking lot and names the two people who will meet after the huddle. Ending on time, every time, builds trust that the meeting will not eat calling hours, and that trust is what keeps people engaged.

Is a weekly meeting enough instead?

A weekly meeting is useful for trends, coaching and lender strategy, but it is too slow for deal flow. By the time a stalled file reaches a weekly review, the merchant may have accepted an offer elsewhere. Most floors benefit from both: a fifteen-minute daily huddle for exceptions and a longer weekly session for patterns and skill development.
