A Branded Merchant Portal for Document Collection

Statements sent as email attachments get lost, mislabelled and copied to places they should never be. A branded portal fixes the handoff at its source.

By the FundPilot teamUpdated October 15, 2026

You asked the merchant for four months of statements on Tuesday. On Wednesday you got two PDFs from the owner, one photo of a screen from the bookkeeper and a reply from the owner's spouse asking which account you meant. On Thursday the third month arrived in a forwarded thread with a different subject line, and your assistant saved it to the wrong folder. Friday, you are still not sure the file is complete.

This is how documents move in a lot of funding shops, and it is a quiet drain on time, accuracy and merchant patience. It also scatters sensitive financial records across inboxes where they were never meant to stay.

A branded merchant portal changes the handoff. The merchant gets one place, under your name, to upload exactly what you need and see what is still missing. Here is why that works and what to look for.

Pre-flight summary

  • Email attachments scatter documents, lose context and leave sensitive copies everywhere.
  • A portal turns a vague document request into a visible checklist for the merchant.
  • Uploads that land directly on the deal remove manual filing and speed up review.
  • Fewer copies, role-based access and an audit trail reduce exposure.
  • Introduce the portal on the call and send the link while the merchant is still on the line.

Leg 01Why email attachments keep failing

Email was never designed as a document intake system. The problems are predictable, and most shops have experienced every one of them.

  • Attachments are split across several threads and senders, so nobody has the full set in one place.
  • File names are meaningless, so someone has to open each PDF to see which month and account it covers.
  • Copies sit in personal inboxes, sent folders and downloads folders long after the deal closes.
  • Large files bounce, and merchants send phone photos instead, which are harder to review.
  • There is no record of what was requested versus what was received, so follow-ups are guesswork.

Leg 02What a portal changes for the merchant

From the merchant's side, a good portal turns a vague request into a checklist. They open a link from your text or email, see your company's name and logo, and find a short list: three most recent business bank statements, a signed application, a copy of a voided check, whatever your file requires. As each item is uploaded, it is marked received.

That visibility matters. A business owner juggling payroll and a busy shift does not want to dig through old messages to remember what you asked for. When the portal shows two of four items received, the next step is obvious and they can do it in a spare minute from their phone.

Branding matters here too. Merchants are rightly careful about where they send bank records. An upload page under your own name and domain is reassuring in a way a generic file-sharing link is not.

A merchant who can see exactly what is missing usually sends it. A merchant who has to guess usually waits.

Leg 03What it changes on your side

Inside the shop, every upload lands directly on the right merchant and deal. Nobody downloads attachments and drags them into folders. The deal record shows what has arrived and what is outstanding, so a manager can see at a glance which files are blocked on documents rather than on underwriting or lender response.

That is also where the next step starts. In FundPilot, statements uploaded through the branded portal sit on the deal where statement underwriting can work through deposits, balances, NSFs and existing positions, with every figure linked back to the source page. The broker reviews the figures against the actual statement instead of retyping them into a spreadsheet.

Status updates get easier to give, too. When a merchant calls to ask where things stand, the answer is on the screen.

Leg 04Security and the paper trail

Bank statements contain account numbers, transaction histories and sometimes personal details. Every place a copy lives is a place it can leak. Collecting documents through a portal into one isolated workspace means fewer stray copies, and access can be governed by role so only the people working the file can open it.

An audit trail adds accountability. If you can see who downloaded a statement and when, you can answer questions from a merchant, a partner or your own counsel with records rather than recollection. Your exact obligations around protecting and retaining financial documents depend on your circumstances, so review them with qualified counsel.

Leg 05Rolling a portal out to merchants

Merchants will not use a portal they do not understand. Introduce it on the call rather than dropping a link cold, and keep the request list short and specific.

  1. Tell the merchant on the call that you will text a secure upload link under your company name.
  2. Send the link while you are still on the phone, so they can confirm it arrived.
  3. Request only the documents your file actually needs, labelled in plain words.
  4. Check the portal before every follow-up so you only ask for what is still missing.
  5. Once the file is complete, let the merchant know, so they are not left wondering.
  6. Stop accepting statements by email except as a fallback, and move any that arrive onto the deal straight away.

Leg 06Applications belong in the same place

The same logic applies to the application itself. A merchant who fills in an application through your branded portal enters their details once, and those details land on the deal without anyone retyping them from a PDF. Combined with document uploads, the portal becomes the single front door for everything the merchant gives you, which is easier for them and much easier to keep tidy on your side.

If you want to see how that front door looks to a merchant, ask for a FundPilot walkthrough of the upload flow on a phone, since that is where most of your merchants will meet it first.

Questions from the floor

Will older or less technical merchants use an upload portal?

Most will if the experience is simple and they hear about it from you first. Keep the request list short, make sure the page works well on a phone, and stay on the line while they open the link the first time. For the occasional merchant who truly cannot use it, accept documents another way and move them onto the deal yourself.

Can merchants upload phone photos of statements?

They can, but downloaded statement files from their bank are much easier to review and less prone to misreading. Ask for the bank's own PDF exports where possible and explain briefly why. Clear source documents also make it easier to verify figures and to notice anything that looks inconsistent with how that bank normally formats statements.

Does a branded portal need its own domain?

It is not strictly required, but a portal under your own domain and branding is more reassuring to merchants and keeps the relationship clearly with your company.
