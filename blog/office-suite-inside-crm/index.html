Why Docs, Sheets and Chat Belong Inside Your CRM

Merchant data does not usually leak from the CRM. It leaks from the spreadsheet someone exported, the chat thread on a personal app and the shared drive nobody audits.

By the FundPilot teamUpdated October 15, 2026

Think about where merchant information actually lives in your shop on an ordinary Wednesday. The CRM holds the deal. But a broker has downloaded statements to work through in a spreadsheet on their laptop. The manager pasted a merchant's revenue figures into a chat app to ask a colleague a question. Someone built a commission tracker in a file-sharing service that was shared by link six months ago and never revisited.

None of those people did anything malicious. They needed to calculate, write or talk, and the CRM did not let them, so they reached for the nearest tool. Every one of those tools now holds sensitive merchant data outside your controls.

The case for an office suite inside the CRM comes down to three things: privacy, context and fewer places for data to slip out. Here is how each plays out on a funding floor.

Pre-flight summary

  • Most data exposure comes from everyday habits, not outside attackers.
  • Docs, sheets and chat inside the CRM inherit its security and access controls.
  • Work attached to the deal survives handoffs, holidays and renewals.
  • The suite must handle real work, including existing Excel files, or people will drift back.
  • Make inside the default and exports a deliberate, logged exception.

Leg 01How data actually leaves a funding shop

When people imagine a data leak, they picture an outside attacker. In practice, much of the exposure in a small financial business comes from ordinary work habits that move data into places nobody is watching.

  • Statements downloaded to personal laptops and never deleted
  • Spreadsheets exported for analysis and emailed around
  • Merchant details pasted into personal or consumer chat apps
  • Shared drive folders with link-sharing left open
  • Notes kept in personal note apps that leave with the employee

Leg 02Privacy: one boundary instead of a dozen

Every separate tool holding merchant data has its own logins, permissions, sharing settings and retention behaviour. Securing all of them consistently is hard, and in a busy shop it rarely happens.

When documents, spreadsheets and chat live inside the CRM, they sit within the same isolated workspace, behind the same two-factor sign-in and the same role-based access as the deals themselves. A broker who can only see their own deals cannot wander into a spreadsheet built from someone else's. Downloads, exports and changes land in the same audit trail, so if a question arises later you can see who took what and when.

Your specific obligations for protecting merchant financial data depend on your circumstances and where you operate, so review them with qualified counsel. Fewer places to secure makes whatever you decide easier to enforce.

You cannot protect data you do not know is there. Keep the work where the controls already are.

Leg 03Context: the work stays attached to the deal

The less obvious benefit is context. Say a broker builds a spreadsheet comparing two offers for a merchant, and a manager leaves a comment in team chat about a concern with the merchant's seasonal dip. If both live in external tools, the next person to open the deal sees neither. If both are attached to the deal, the history is complete.

This matters most at handoffs: a broker goes on holiday, a file moves to a closer, a renewal comes up a year later. The person picking it up should not have to hunt through someone else's drive and message history to understand what happened.

It matters in meetings, too. When a manager and two brokers jump on a quick video call to talk through a difficult file, they can have the deal, the statement figures and the offer comparison sheet open in the same place, and any decision they reach can be noted on the deal straight afterwards. Nobody has to remember to copy a summary from one app into another, which in practice means nobody does.

Leg 04What the suite needs to handle

An office suite inside a CRM is only useful if people can actually do their work in it. If it is too limited, they will go back to their old tools within a week. FundPilot includes docs, spreadsheets that open Excel files, presentations, whiteboards, forms, file storage with version history, team chat and video calls, all inside the CRM.

Opening existing Excel files matters more than it sounds. Many shops have years of worksheets, calculators and lender grids built in spreadsheets. Being able to open and work on them without exporting is what makes moving them inside realistic. Version history matters too: when a commission sheet changes, you want to see what changed and who changed it.

Leg 05Moving the team over without a fight

People are attached to their tools, and an instruction to stop using them rarely works on its own. A gradual, practical approach tends to go better.

  1. Start with the riskiest habit, usually statements and spreadsheets on personal laptops.
  2. Move shared worksheets and templates into the CRM's file storage first.
  3. Set up team chat channels that mirror how the floor already talks, such as by team or by lender.
  4. Hold one short session showing brokers how to attach a doc or sheet to a deal.
  5. Agree a date after which merchant data should not be shared through outside tools.
  6. Review the audit trail after a month to see where exports still happen and why.

Leg 06Where outside tools still have a place

This is not an argument that nothing should ever leave the CRM. Signed agreements may go to an accountant, and some lenders require submissions in particular formats. The aim is to make inside the default and outside a deliberate, logged exception rather than a habit. When an export does happen, it should be because someone chose to, with a record of it, not because the CRM could not do the job.

A simple rule of thumb helps brokers decide in the moment: if the information is about a specific merchant, it stays inside. If it is general, such as a training note or a lunch order, use whatever you like.

Questions from the floor

Will brokers really give up their usual chat app?

Some will resist at first, particularly for casual conversation. Focus on work that involves merchant data: discussions about specific deals, figures and documents should happen inside the CRM, where they are attached to the record and covered by its controls. Casual chat matters less. Once brokers see that deal conversations are easier to find later, adoption usually follows.

Can we still use our existing Excel calculators?

In FundPilot, the spreadsheet tool opens Excel files, so existing worksheets and calculators can be stored and used inside the CRM rather than on individual laptops. Check complex workbooks after moving them, especially ones with macros or external links, and keep a copy of the original until you are confident everything behaves as expected.

Does keeping everything in one system create a single point of failure?

It concentrates your data, which is why the controls around that system matter so much: an isolated workspace, two-factor sign-in, role-based access, version history and a full audit trail. Ask any vendor about backups and recovery as part of your due diligence. One well-protected system is generally easier to defend than many loosely managed ones.
