Running a Remote or Hybrid Funding Team

The floor used to be a room. Now it might be a room plus six kitchens. How to keep calls, files, accountability and culture working when brokers are not side by side.

Some of the energy of a funding floor came from the room itself. You heard a colleague close a deal two desks over. A manager walked past, saw a broker stuck on a call, and slipped them a note. A new hire learned by overhearing a hundred conversations a day. When part or all of the team works from home, that ambient learning and oversight disappears unless you rebuild it on purpose.

Remote and hybrid funding teams can work very well. Brokers avoid commutes, you can hire outside your city, and focused people often dial more productively at home. But the shops that make it work treat remote as a design problem, not a perk. They set up calling, data security, accountability and team life deliberately. Here is what that looks like.

Pre-flight summary

  • Give remote brokers business numbers and calls logged to the deal.
  • Measure outcomes and leading indicators, not screen time.
  • Replace overhearing with recordings, reviews and remote shadowing.
  • Keep merchant files inside the system with two-factor and role-based access.
  • Run a hybrid team as if everyone were remote.

Leg 01Calls from anywhere, logged everywhere

The first requirement is that a remote broker's calls work exactly like an office broker's. That means business numbers rather than personal mobiles, caller ID that merchants recognise, and every call, text and recording attached to the deal, not trapped on someone's phone.

Personal phones create real problems: merchant conversations you cannot see, text threads that leave with the broker if they leave the company, and no way to coach from recordings. Browser-based calling solves most of this. FundPilot's built-in dialer runs in the browser and on desktop, iPad and iPhone, with two-way texting and local presence caller ID, and logs every call to the deal whether the broker is in the office or at their kitchen table.

Leg 02Accountability without micromanaging

The tempting response to remote work is surveillance: screen monitoring, keystroke counts, mandatory webcam all day. It signals distrust, good brokers leave, and it measures the wrong things anyway. A broker can be at their screen for eight hours and move nothing.

Measure outcomes and leading indicators instead, exactly as you would in the office. Contact rate, applications, document completion, time in stage. If those numbers are healthy, the broker is working. If they slip, you have a specific, fair conversation to have, grounded in data the broker can see too.

Say a remote broker's dials look normal but their contact rate has fallen. Before assuming anything, check the simple things: are they dialing at the right times for their merchants' time zones, is their caller ID set correctly, has their lead allocation shifted to an older source? The answer is often mechanical.

Watch outcomes, not webcams. A broker who moves deals from home is doing the job.

Leg 03Rebuilding the learning the room used to provide

Overhearing is how many brokers learned the trade. Remote teams need a substitute. Recordings and transcripts are the closest thing, and they are better in some ways: you can choose the best calls rather than whatever happens to be audible.

Build a short weekly ritual around them. Pick two or three recorded calls that went well, share the transcript summaries in team chat, and spend ten minutes in a video call discussing what made them work. New hires can shadow live calls remotely too, by joining as a silent listener where your setup and notice practices allow it.

  • A weekly call-of-the-week review over video
  • A shared library of strong recordings sorted by situation
  • Remote shadowing sessions booked for every new hire
  • An open chat channel for quick questions during live deals
  • Fortnightly one-to-ones with each remote broker

Leg 04Protecting merchant data outside the office

Funding files contain bank statements, tax identifiers, owner IDs and contact details. In an office you control the network, the machines and who walks past a screen. At home you control far less, so the system itself has to do more of the work.

Keep files inside the CRM instead of in email attachments and downloads folders. Require two-factor sign-in for everyone. Use role-based access so brokers see their own deals and not the whole book. Keep an audit trail of exports and downloads so unusual activity is visible. FundPilot provides each of these, and it keeps documents in file storage with version history rather than scattered across laptops.

Set clear written rules too: no printing merchant files at home, no forwarding statements to personal email, screens locked when stepping away. And confirm with qualified counsel what your privacy and data-protection obligations are, since they can apply regardless of where your staff sit.

Leg 05Keeping a hybrid floor from splitting in two

Hybrid teams face a specific risk: the in-office group becomes the real team and the remote group becomes the satellite. Office brokers get the quick hallway advice, the first look at hot leads, and the manager's attention. Remote brokers notice and drift.

Run everything as if the whole team were remote. Huddles happen on video even for people in the room, lead routing follows a visible rule, and decisions get written in team chat rather than made in the corridor.

  1. Hold every team meeting on video, with in-office staff joining from their own screens
  2. Route leads by a published rule, never by who is physically present
  3. Post deal-related decisions in the team channel
  4. Rotate who gets in-person coaching time
  5. Meet in person on a regular schedule if distance allows

Leg 06Culture is the small, repeated things

Culture on a funding floor is not the pizza on Friday. It is how people treat each other when a deal falls through, whether help is offered without being asked, and whether wins are noticed. Remotely, those moments need a channel.

Celebrate fundings in team chat with a sentence about what the broker did well, not just the amount. Make it normal to ask for help in the open channel. Keep the cameras-on rule for meetings, not for the whole day. Questions from the floor

Can new brokers start fully remote?

They can, but it takes more structure. A new remote broker needs a named mentor, booked shadowing sessions, a practice environment before live calls, and more frequent check-ins during the first month. If you have an office, a few in-person days at the start often speed things up. Either way, recordings and transcripts become the main teaching tool, so make sure they are easy to find.

How do I handle a remote broker whose numbers are slipping?

Start with curiosity rather than accusation. Look at the specific leading indicator that moved, check mechanical causes like dialing times or lead allocation, and listen to a few recent calls. Then talk privately on video. Most dips have a fixable cause. If performance stays low after support and clear expectations, handle it the same way you would for an in-office broker.

Should remote brokers record calls?

Recording is valuable for coaching and dispute resolution, but consent and notice rules vary by jurisdiction and can depend on where each party to the call is located. Remote teams may call across many states, which adds complexity. Confirm your recording practices and notice wording with qualified counsel and apply them consistently, whether a broker is at home or in the office.

What equipment should a remote broker have?

A reliable computer with current security updates, a good headset, a stable internet connection, and a quiet space for calls. A tablet or phone with the CRM app is useful for working away from the desk. Avoid letting brokers use shared family computers for merchant files. Some shops provide equipment so they can manage security settings centrally.
