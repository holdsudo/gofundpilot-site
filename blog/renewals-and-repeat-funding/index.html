Renewals: Building a Book of Repeat Merchants

The merchant you funded last spring already knows you. Whether they come back depends on how you track their paydown and how you talk to them when the time is right.

By the FundPilot teamUpdated October 15, 2026

  3. Time the call around the merchant, not the quota
  4. Have the responsible version of the conversation
  5. Turn renewals into a weekly routine
  6. Service between deals is what earns the renewal
  7. Questions

New files cost a lot to find. Leads, dial time, applications that go nowhere, statements that never arrive. A renewal skips most of that. The merchant has already been through underwriting with you, has a payment history the funder can see, and, if the first deal went well, already trusts your floor.

Yet many shops treat renewals as something that happens by accident: a merchant calls back, or a broker happens to remember a name. A deliberate renewal practice changes that. It rests on two things, knowing exactly where each funded merchant stands, and having renewal conversations that are timed well and genuinely in the merchant's interest.

Pre-flight summary

  • Track paydown percentage and pace on every funded deal, updated regularly.
  • Record each funder's renewal criteria the way you record submission guidelines.
  • Time renewal conversations around the merchant's situation, not monthly targets.
  • Always show the net cash after payoff and fees, and say so when more funding would not help.
  • Make the funded book a weekly review with its own pipeline stages.

Leg 01Track paydown on every funded deal

You cannot time a renewal conversation without knowing how far along the merchant is. For each funded deal, keep the total payback, the amount paid to date and the resulting percentage, updated on a regular schedule. Funders typically share balance information with the broker or make it available on request; the source and frequency vary.

Paydown percentage on its own is not enough. Note the pace too. A merchant who has paid 50 percent in the expected time is in a different position from one who reached 50 percent after several reconciliation adjustments because sales dipped. The first may be ready to talk. The second may need a different conversation entirely, or none at all yet.

In FundPilot, funded deals carry their payment schedules and payoffs on the same ledger used for syndication and commissions, so paydown sits alongside the rest of the deal history rather than in a separate tracker.

Leg 02Know what renewal eligibility usually looks like

Funders set their own renewal criteria, and they differ. Common factors include how much of the current balance has been paid, whether payments have been consistent, and whether the merchant's revenue still supports additional funding. Some funders will consider a renewal earlier than others.

Record each funder's renewal guidelines on the lender record, just like submission guidelines. When a funded merchant approaches a funder's threshold, you will know which funder to speak to and what they will want to see.

  • Percentage of the current advance paid down
  • Payment consistency, including any returned payments
  • Current revenue compared with revenue at original funding
  • Any new positions opened since funding
  • Updated statements, usually the most recent months

Leg 03Time the call around the merchant, not the quota

The best time to raise a renewal is when the merchant could plausibly benefit from it and is likely to qualify. Calling too early wastes their time and yours. Calling only when your month is short of target is how renewals turn into pressure.

A practical approach is to set a review point, for example when a deal reaches a paydown level your main funders tend to consider, and to make that review a look at the merchant's situation rather than an automatic pitch. Pull fresh statements, check the trend, and ask whether more capital would actually help this business right now.

Campaigns can help with the outreach side. A text or email sequence that respects local time and stops as soon as the merchant replies is a gentle way to reopen the conversation, followed by a call from the broker who knows the file.

A renewal raised because the merchant could use it builds a book. One raised because your month is short builds complaints.

Leg 04Have the responsible version of the conversation

Renewals carry a specific risk: the merchant can end up paying fees and cost on top of an existing balance without fully realizing it. If a renewal pays off the remaining balance on the current advance, the new net amount the merchant receives is smaller than the headline figure, and that needs to be stated plainly.

Say a merchant is approved for a $50,000 renewal and still has $18,000 left to deliver on the current advance. These numbers are hypothetical. After the $18,000 payoff and, say, a $1,000 fee, roughly $31,000 reaches their account. That $31,000, set against the new total payback, is what the merchant should be weighing. Present it that way, alongside the payment schedule, and let them decide.

Also listen for signs that more funding is not the answer. If deposits are falling, the merchant is already stretched, or they want capital mainly to cover the existing payments, a renewal may make things worse. Saying so is part of the job.

  1. Pull current statements and compare revenue with the original file.
  2. Confirm the remaining balance on the current advance with the funder.
  3. Calculate the net cash the merchant would actually receive after payoff and fees.
  4. Lay out the new payback, payment and estimated pace side by side with the current deal.
  5. Ask what the funding is for and whether the payment fits their balances.
  6. Record the conversation and the merchant's decision on the deal.

Leg 05Turn renewals into a weekly routine

Renewals work best as a standing item, not an occasional project. Each week, a manager or designated broker reviews the funded book: which deals crossed a review point, which merchants have been contacted, and which have said not now. Each of those merchants gets a next step and a date.

Keep renewal deals in their own pipeline stages so they do not get lost among new files. Stages such as review due, statements requested, submitted for renewal and declined for now make the funded book visible at a glance.

Leg 06Service between deals is what earns the renewal

A merchant who never heard from you between funding and renewal has little reason to call you rather than whoever is dialing them that week. Small contacts matter: answering a question about their balance, helping them reach the funder when a payment issue comes up, sending documents promptly when asked.

Log those contacts on the deal. When a different broker picks up the renewal, or the original broker leaves, that history tells them who this merchant is and how the relationship has gone. Questions from the floor

When should a broker first raise a renewal?

It depends on the funder's renewal criteria and the merchant's situation. Many shops set a review point at a paydown level their main funders typically consider, then look at fresh statements before deciding whether to raise it at all. The conversation should start because the merchant may benefit and is likely to qualify, not simply because a number was reached or a target is behind.

What does net funding mean on a renewal?

On a renewal that pays off an existing balance, net funding is the cash the merchant actually receives after that payoff and any fees are deducted. It is usually much smaller than the approved amount. Presenting the net figure clearly, alongside the new total payback and payment schedule, is essential so the merchant understands what the renewal really gives them and what it costs.

Is it ever right to advise against a renewal?

Yes. If a merchant's revenue is falling, their balances are already thin, or they mainly want new funding to cover existing payments, additional capital may deepen the problem. Telling them so honestly is responsible practice and often earns more long-term trust than a funded deal would. You can note the review on the deal and agree to revisit when their situation improves.
